ccy-teams — see the agent teams on this machine and look after them: each team's
homeserver, its members, the people in its room, and the ccy sessions in its seats. It is
to teams what ccy-sessions is to sessions (Plan 00161, Plan 00177; docs/agent-bus.md,
"Getting started").

A team is declared in the untracked host_vars localhost.yml (agent_bus_teams) and made by
play-agent-bus.yml. A ccy session joins one with `ccy --teams <seat>@<team>`, an LXC
container's agent through agent_bus_lxc_members (ccy-teams lxc-add writes the entry). ccy-teams holds no team logic of its own:
it acts through agent-bus, agent-bus-install, ccy and the session registry. It edits the
host_vars only to add a team (create) or to declare one absent (remove), and every such
change is applied at once: it runs play-agent-bus.yml for that team alone (its own
shebang, through run.bash's single-play mode), so no team is left declared but not built.
An LXC container's agent is added the same way (lxc-add), with no hand edit of the host_vars.

Each team is a row (its homeserver up, down or not installed yet, its port, and APPLIED:
whether what runs is what the host_vars declare, DESIGN.md D65), followed by its members:
  ccy                    a seat of a ccy checkout, with the ccy session holding it and
                         whether that session is started again after a reboot
  lxc, host, docker, vm  a member of that type; a ccy member whose checkout is not
                         known here (no session or registry record names it) is "ccy"
                         with no checkout
  human                  a person in the team room: in it or not, and how many Element
                         sessions they are signed in with
Enter on any row opens a menu of everything that can be done to it, in words, each with its
shortcut beside it (Esc goes back to the list); the picker's header names the row's actions
too (? there shows every key, explained). The shortcuts:
  Enter    a menu for the row. On a seat it begins with attaching its session (as
           ccy-sessions does); on a person who is signed in, with opening Element; on a
           person who never has been, with signing in (a password, shown once); on a team,
           with opening Element
  Ctrl-N   start a new ccy session in a free or new seat of the row's team, here
  Ctrl-P   a new Element password for the person on the row; it asks first, saying how
           many of their Element sessions it signs out (none: no question)
  Ctrl-E   open the team in Element (setting its profile up first, when it has none);
           Element starts apart from ccy-teams, which stays on the list. Each team is its
           own Element window, because each team is its own Matrix server and an Element
           window holds one account; the menu of a team row has Open every team in
           Element, a window each (no shortcut), which opens every team with a profile
  Ctrl-U   on a team row: update every team that is out of date or not built (never one
           to take down)
  Ctrl-R   turn the seat's session's restore after a reboot off or on
  Ctrl-X   remove a seat, park a member, or take a team down (each after a yes/no)
  Tab      the last row, Running sessions: every session ccy's tmux started, its teams
           (seat, role) and its state. Enter on one lists, in words, what can be done to
           it: Add to a team, Remove from <team>, Change role in <team>, Move to another
           team, Attach, and Repair when its seat keeper has stopped. Each is
           `ccy-teams session ...` below, run for you, with its question and its steps.
           Back to the teams, or Esc, comes back
A key works only on a row whose menu has it.
The menu of a team row also has Shut down every session of <team> (no shortcut): the same as
`ccy-teams shutdown <team>` below.
The menu of a team row also has Edit settings (no shortcut): every field of the team's entry
with its value. Each editable one is checked as it is typed; Save and apply writes the changes
to the host_vars in one edit and applies the team, as ccy-teams apply does. The coordinator
changes at once; the name, port, listen addresses, server name, forge API and removed people
are fixed. A person removed is deactivated for good and their name can never be added back.
A team has at most one coordinator, an agent that takes the humans' requests, dispatches them
to the workers and is copied on what workers tell humans. Its seat's MEMBER shows
"(coordinator)". The menu of a seat has Make it the coordinator (<team> has none), Stop
being coordinator, or, on a team that has a coordinator, Replace the coordinator, listed
last (no shortcut): a takeover names the current coordinator and whether it runs, says it
loses the role, and defaults to no. Each asks first and runs agent-bus set-role as root.
  Esc, q   leave
Every row's menu also has Add an LXC container's agent to <team> (no shortcut): the same as
`ccy-teams lxc-add <team>` below.
With no team on this machine, the picker offers to create one; with a team out of date, it
offers to update it first; with a team still holding the old default path_prefixes, it
offers once to remove them. Run in a checkout of a team's repositories, with no session of
that checkout in the team, it offers to start one there (a bare ccy --teams, which lists the
teams); run in a GitHub checkout no team covers, it says so and offers to create a team for
it, or to start a session there that chooses its teams (ccy --teams).

Reading the members needs root (the team's admin token is root's). play-agent-bus.yml
installs a sudoers rule that lets you run exactly `agent-bus list` and `agent-bus human
devices` without a password; where that rule is not installed yet and sudo needs a
password, the picker says why before sudo asks, and stops, saying so, when sudo is
refused. --list never asks: without sudo it shows the members as "need sudo", and, as with
any part of the table it could not read, it still prints the rest but exits 1.

Usage:
  ccy-teams                        the picker (needs a terminal)
  ccy-teams -l|--list              print the same table, with a heading, and exit
                                   (colour only on a terminal, and never with NO_COLOR)
  ccy-teams create [--org ORG | --repo OWNER/REPO...] [--team T] [--human NAME]...
                   [--allow-from CIDR]... [--description TEXT] [--yes]
                                   add a team to agent_bus_teams in the host_vars, with
                                   its Element profile in agent_bus_element_teams, and
                                   install both. On a terminal it asks (with gum) for what
                                   the flags leave out: what the team works on (one
                                   repository, the default in a checkout, which it names;
                                   everything one GitHub owner owns; or several
                                   repositories of any owners, each one you can push to),
                                   all on any branch; the name (the repository's or the
                                   owner's, by default); the people in the team room (you,
                                   by default); subnets of other machines (LXC is automatic). It shows a
                                   summary and asks once, then writes the entries and runs
                                   play-agent-bus.yml and play-agent-bus-element.yml, and
                                   says it is done only when both are applied. Then it
                                   offers each person's sign-in, opens Element, and, in a
                                   checkout of the team's, starts a ccy session there.
                                   --yes asks and offers nothing: give --org or --repo,
                                   --team and --human. The port is chosen for you
  ccy-teams lxc-add <team>         add an LXC container's agent to a team, on a terminal only,
                                   with no hand edit of the host_vars. It lists this host's
                                   running containers (as root: sudo asks), then asks, with
                                   gum, for the container, its user (the container's ordinary
                                   users are suggested), the checkout inside it (typed on a
                                   line, Tab completing a directory name; those with a GitHub
                                   remote in the user's home, /var/www, /srv and /opt are
                                   suggested, ccy projects first) and the role (worker first; the
                                   coordinator on a team with none, or on a team that has one a
                                   takeover that names it, on No), each question naming the
                                   team, the step, the container and the user. The
                                   handle's repo, host and seat are never asked: they come from
                                   the checkout as ccy derives them (repo from its origin, host
                                   from .claude/ccy/ccy.env.local else local, the next free
                                   seat), read from this host's side as plain files, no git run.
                                   Each answer is checked by declare.py as it is given, and
                                   asked again when refused. It shows a summary with the
                                   derived handle and asks once, then writes the
                                   agent_bus_lxc_members entry and runs play-agent-bus.yml for
                                   the team, which joins the member (the user must already
                                   exist and be logged in to gh in the container; the play
                                   stops, naming it, when not). It ends by printing what is
                                   left inside the container: the kit's agent-bus-claude as that
                                   user in the checkout (a session already running there is
                                   named, to be started again), and a live pingbus check.
                                   Hand-editing agent_bus_lxc_members still works
  ccy-teams apply <team> | --all   run play-agent-bus.yml for the team (or every team
                                   out of date or not built), and play-agent-bus-element.yml
                                   for those with an Element profile, then check each now
                                   matches its declaration. --all never takes a team
                                   down: a team declared absent, or built but no longer
                                   declared, needs your decision (ccy-teams remove)
  ccy-teams remove <team>          take a team down, on a terminal only: it says what is
                                   lost, offers a backup first, and keeps the team's data
                                   unless you choose to delete it and type its name
  ccy-teams backup <team>          back the team's homeserver up now
  ccy-teams restore <team> [<n>] [--yes]
                                   restore backup <n> after a yes/no (--yes: none);
                                   without <n>, choose one from the list
  ccy-teams shutdown <team> [--yes]
                                   end every session of the team on this host: it lists
                                   them, and the members it cannot reach from here (an
                                   LXC container's agent, another machine), then asks
                                   (default No; with no terminal, --yes is required).
                                   Each session gets /exit typed into its tmux pane
                                   (Escape first when busy) and is stopped only if it
                                   has not ended within 60 s; then each of its seats must
                                   be free. One line per member: exited cleanly, stopped,
                                   failed or not shut down; exit 1 unless all ended. The
                                   homeserver keeps running. Nothing goes on the bus
  ccy-teams for-checkout          print the team(s) that work on this checkout's GitHub
                                   OWNER/REPO (an organisation team by its owner, unless
                                   it excludes the repository), one per line on stdout, the
                                   rest on stderr. With none, it fails saying so. For scripts;
                                   ccy chooses with choose-teams
  ccy-teams choose-teams --type=podman|host [<list>]
                                   the launcher's team chooser, on a terminal only. With a
                                   <list> (SEAT@TEAM or a team alone, comma-separated) it
                                   chooses the seat of each team named alone and prints the
                                   canonical list. With none, it shows every team on this
                                   machine with what it is for and whether it covers this
                                   checkout (covers, would add, excludes), and offers
                                   creating one. A team that does not cover the checkout is
                                   offered the repository in its scope (edits the host_vars,
                                   runs play-agent-bus.yml for it, no homeserver restart), or
                                   joined as it is, or left out. Then the seat. Prints
                                   SEAT@TEAM[,...] on stdout, the rest on stderr. Leaving any
                                   screen prints nothing and succeeds. With --type=host (cc)
                                   it also asks, once per user, whether a host seat may run
                                   beside your Element session, and records the answer in
                                   ~/.config/pingbus/element-accepted; declining ends the
                                   run (exit 1); with no terminal and no valid record
                                   (yours, mode 600, in your closed folder) it exits 78
                                   before any seat is made. ccy --teams and cc --teams call it
  ccy-teams choose-teams --type=host --bus
                                   only that once-per-user question, for cc --bus: a session
                                   on no team that you add to teams later (ccy-teams session
                                   add). Prints nothing; declining ends the run (exit 1)
  ccy-teams record-seats <session> <seats>
                                   put the seats (a canonical --teams list) into the record of
                                   the session, so its restore replays them. ccy calls it, naming
                                   its own session, when Quick Launch brings a saved team back
  ccy-teams internal membership-init <session> <checkout> <seats>
                                   write generation 1 of the session's desired agent team
                                   bus membership (<seats>: the canonical --teams list, empty
                                   for none) into $XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/ccy/membership/<session>/,
                                   private to you, and print that directory, which ccy mounts
                                   read-only into the container. ccy calls it at each launch
                                   that carries the bus kit (Plan 00203)
  ccy-teams internal membership-init --kind=cc <session> <checkout> <seats>
                                   the same for cc --teams and cc --bus, into
                                   $XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/pingbus/membership/<session>/, which the
                                   session reads directly (it runs as you, so it could write it
                                   too); the seats also go into the session's record. cc calls
                                   it at each such launch
  ccy-teams internal restart-args <session>
                                   read a relaunch's arguments, NUL separated, on stdin and
                                   print them with the --teams of <session>'s record ("" for an
                                   unnamed launch: unchanged), so a supervisor restart replays a
                                   membership changed while the session ran. ccy calls it
  ccy-teams internal claim-container-name <project> <suffix> <owner-pid>
                                   print the lowest free container name, <project>_<suffix>
                                   then _1, _2 ..., and claim it for process <owner-pid> in
                                   $XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/ccy/container-names/, so launches started
                                   together never choose the same name. A name is taken while
                                   a container has it (the engine is $CONTAINER_ENGINE, else
                                   podman) or while another running process claimed it; the
                                   owner's own earlier claim is given up. ccy calls it at each
                                   launch
  ccy-teams session list
  ccy-teams session add <session> <team>[/<seat>] [--role=worker|coordinator] [--demote-current] [--yes]
  ccy-teams session remove <session> <team> [--coordinator=demote|hand-to=<seat>] [--park] [--yes]
  ccy-teams session move <session> <from-team> <to-team>[/<seat>] [--coordinator=...] [--yes]
  ccy-teams session role <session>|<handle> <team> worker|coordinator [--demote-current] [--yes]
  ccy-teams session repair <session> [--yes]
                                   Only sessions started through ccy's tmux (listed by ccy-sessions), and cc sessions started with
                                   --teams or --bus, can be changed while they run. list shows
                                   the running sessions, their teams, roles and state. add puts
                                   the running session into the team, in the seat given or the
                                   lowest free one, and waits for it to hold the seat; a session
                                   started on no team relaunches once, at its next idle moment,
                                   to accept team messages. remove releases the seat, clears its
                                   sync position, expires its listening status and posts its
                                   goodbye; the token stays valid (remove does not revoke). move
                                   is add then remove; role sets the seat's role and waits for
                                   the session to be told, or, given a member's handle, sets the
                                   role of a member of any kind (ccy, cc, LXC, host, docker, vm);
                                   repair restarts a stopped seat keeper.
                                   One line per step on stderr (✓, ✗, or - for already so); asks
                                   first, --yes required with no terminal. Exit 75 for a seat
                                   held or named elsewhere, or another change in progress (one
                                   change at a time per session, one seat allocation at a time
                                   per checkout), 78 for a session that cannot be changed while
                                   it runs. ccy-teams session --help says more
  ccy-teams choose-seat <team>     choose which seat this session takes in the team (gum), then
                                   whether the seat is the team's coordinator (offered plainly
                                   when the team has none; when it has one, a takeover that
                                   names it, defaulting to no), a worker, or stops being the
                                   coordinator; prints SEAT@TEAM on stdout, the
                                   rest on stderr. Leaving the chooser prints nothing and
                                   succeeds. The role is asked only for a seat that exists, and
                                   sudo only when you change it. choose-teams uses the same
                                   chooser for each team named alone
  ccy-teams describe-seats [--prefix TEXT] <list>
                                   print each seat of a --teams list followed by its role,
                                   (coordinator) or (worker), on one line after TEXT: what
                                   Quick Launch shows as the previous team. ccy calls it
  ccy-teams describe-seats --block <list>
                                   print "This session is on N teams:" and, per seat, its
                                   team, seat, role and what the team is for: the block a
                                   launch shows once its seats are taken
  ccy-teams -h|--help              this text

Backups stay on this machine (docs/agent-bus.md, "Backups are the root of trust").
The APPLIED column, from `declare.py status` against the installer's stamps (readable
without sudo): up to date, out of date (config, software or both), not built, removed,
or needs your decision (declared absent but still built, or built but not declared).
Exit 64 for a usage mistake, 130 when cancelled, 1 for a refusal or a failure.

Runs on the HOST, as the desktop user.
