## 17:59 · finding · P1   — live episode findings and the dGPU wake lead

Live lag episode investigated in-session; system-level causes ruled out, the dGPU runtime-PM wake is the new lead.

**First episode (reported "a moment ago", about 17:30–17:38 UTC).** The journal
showed nothing input-related: only the recurring `Can't update stage views`
warnings from the dash-to-dock and blur extensions (bursts of 40–190 per minute
between 17:11 and 17:37), a chat client's `Buggy client ... working around`
geometry warnings, and one `nvidia-modeset: Correcting number of heads` line at
the moment a new terminal window probed Vulkan (which also opened and closed the
evdi DisplayLink node).

**Second episode, captured live (about 17:40 UTC).** Measured while the owner
reported active lag:

```
pressure cpu/memory/io  some avg10 = 0.00 / 0.00 / 0.00
gnome-shell             ~12% CPU, KMS thread ~3%, input thread ~1%
display                 internal eDP only, 3072x1920@120, i915 ~200 IRQ/s
dGPU                    runtime-suspended
package throttle        no change during sampling; ~44C
ACPI GPE 0x6E (EC)      0/s during the episode (~2.6/s long-run average)
touchpad (IRQ ELAN)     ~170 reports/s
i2c_designware IRQ      ~5,500/s = ~31 per report; since-boot ratio also 31
```

The I2C interrupt rate looked like a storm, but its ratio to touchpad reports
equals the since-boot average, so it is the normal cost of I2C-HID on this
controller, not a fault. No i2c/hid/elan kernel messages were logged that day.

What the capture could NOT show: per-event timing through libinput and mutter.
It proved the kernel was receiving touchpad reports at a normal rate and that the
machine was idle, which leaves libinput or the compositor. That gap is why
`triage.bash --capture` now records libinput event timing.

**TrackPoint.** The owner reports it is far too fast to use. Everything is at
defaults (kernel sensitivity 128, GNOME pointingstick speed 0.0, no
`/etc/libinput`). The shipped libinput 1.31.3 Lenovo quirks set
`AttrTrackpointMultiplier` for many ThinkPads (P14s Gen 1: 0.4) but have no
section for the P14s Gen 5, so this model runs at a multiplier of 1.0.

**dGPU wake (new lead, H5).** While the triage script was being tested, the dGPU
was twice caught in `resuming`, both times alongside two `kacpi_notify` kworkers
at about 20% CPU. A 100ms sysfs watch then caught an unprompted wake:

```
suspended -> resuming   +0.000s
resuming  -> active     +1.588s
active    -> suspending +20.3s
suspending-> suspended  +0.8s
```

gnome-shell holds `/dev/nvidia0` and `/dev/nvidia-modeset`, as do the GTK4
terminal and Firefox's media-decoder processes. A 1.6s resume that the
compositor waits on would look exactly like a pointer freeze. This is not yet
correlated with an episode. The capture mode now logs transitions, so the next
episode will confirm or refute it.

**Baseline.** A 30s capture with no lag reported gave 1,776 touchpad motion
events: 1,624 gaps under 20ms, 113 at 20–50ms, 12 at 50–100ms, 14 at 100–300ms
and 13 at 300ms or more (finger lifts). A lagging capture is compared with this.
During that capture the ACPI SCI fired in bursts of about 22 roughly every 5s
once the dGPU had suspended. Noted, not yet interpreted.
