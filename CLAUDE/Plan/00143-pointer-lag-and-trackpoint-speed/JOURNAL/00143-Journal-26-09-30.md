## 08:30 · decision · T1.2   — record continuously, correlate after

Third episode, reported after it had ended. Post-hoc triage found nothing, so a long-running capture recorder is now the method.

The owner reported a glitch that had already cleared. A snapshot taken straight
afterwards showed pressure 0, no kernel input/GPU/ACPI lines in 30 minutes, the
dGPU `active` (held by gnome-shell and a Firefox media-decoder process), and the
usual bursts of `Can't update stage views` warnings (up to 310/min). Nothing
localised the episode, and its exact time is unknown.

Decision: episodes are short and reported late, and neither the kernel nor
mutter logs the signals that matter. So post-hoc triage cannot localise them.
Instead, `triage.bash --capture 7200` runs as a bounded background recorder. Its
per-second table already separates the hypotheses. `touchpad/s` staying high
while `i915/s` falls to near 0 means the compositor stopped presenting (H1/H5).
A `resuming` span in `dgpu-transitions.txt` at that moment supports H5.
`touchpad/s` falling means the device path (H2). When the owner reports the next
glitch time, read those rows and the raw libinput events for that window.

## 09:02 · finding · T1.2   — 30-minute recording; resting-finger confound

The first 30-minute recording caught no stall. The resting-finger confound was found and fixed, and events now carry wall-clock time.

The recorder ran 09:29:55–09:59:41 before the harness's background time limit
ended it (not the intended 2 hours). What it showed:

- Two unprompted dGPU wakes, 09:30:55 (1.75s to `active`) and 09:31:56 (1.65s),
  matching F11. After the second wake the dGPU stayed `active` for the rest of
  the recording, so later stalls could not have been wakes.
- 47 seconds with the touchpad saturated (~148 IRQ/s) and i915 at ~9/s. That
  looked like the lag signature, but it is a confound: a finger RESTING on the
  pad keeps reporting at full rate while the cursor does not move, so nothing
  needs presenting.
- With libinput motions aligned to the table (alignment inferred at −9s by
  correlation, not measured), only 7 of 312 finger-moving seconds had i915 < 40,
  scattered and never a sustained run. No episode is evident in the window, and
  none was reported for it.

Changes: libinput lines are now prefixed with the wall-clock second, so the join
is exact rather than inferred, and the report gains a "compositor stall seconds"
section that counts only seconds with real motion. The join was checked on
synthetic input: a moving second with low i915 is flagged, and a resting-finger
second is excluded.

Background runs from an agent session are capped at about 30 minutes, so long
recordings are for the owner to run in a spare terminal.
