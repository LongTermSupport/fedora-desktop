## 12:18 · action · T1.2   — Ctrl+C now stops a capture

The owner hit a third lag episode (about 11:33–11:42) and started `--capture 600`
at 11:41:59. Ctrl+C did not stop it, so the process groups were killed by hand.

Causes, reproduced with a real `^C` through a pty:
- bash starts every `&` job in a script with SIGINT ignored, so the recorders
  never saw it
- `timeout` moves into its own process group, and sudo runs libinput on its own pty
- `probe-pointer.bash` had no trap, so it died and left the recorders running
- `triage.bash`'s log drain then waited on a `tee` the orphans still held open

Fix: `probe-pointer.bash` tracks each recorder's PID and stops, waits for and
cleans up after them on INT/TERM/HUP/EXIT. `triage.bash` runs the capture leg in
the background and stops it through `plan_on_cleanup`. Verified: `^C` during capture
and during the snapshot exits in about 1s with no survivors; TERM on the
`triage.bash` PID does the same; normal runs finish rc=0.

Still open: `_planlib.inc.bash` waits forever on anything that holds the log pipe.
That needs a follow-up in the library.

## 13:39 · finding · T1.2   — baseline for the "motion but no cursor update" signature

The owner left `--capture 28800` running from 13:53:12 BST (run `20260930-135312`)
and reported no lag through about 14:36. Per-second join of libinput `POINTER_MOTION`
against the display trace's cursor-plane updates over 2,604 seconds:

- 774 seconds had at least 20 libinput motion events.
- 68 of those had cursor-plane updates at 10% of the motion count or less.
- The longest run of such seconds is **2** (13:56:20–21).
- High touchpad-IRQ seconds with no motion: 225 were scrolling and 157
  were a resting finger or palm. Raw `tp-irq` against `cursor` therefore over-reports.

So isolated "motion without cursor update" seconds occur in normal, lag-free use.
Candidate benign causes, not yet separated: pushing against a screen edge, a
software-cursor fallback drawn on the primary plane, and an app hiding the cursor.
An episode should show as a **sustained** run of such seconds (a run of minutes, not 1–2s).
Analyses of an in-episode capture must use libinput motion, not `tp-irq`, as the
denominator and look for runs, not single seconds.
