## 12:15 · finding · —   — Esc swallowed in a menu: fzf joins Esc to the next key

The host agent's Esc finding (a row menu stays open when Down follows Esc within about 50 ms, or both come in one `tmux send-keys`) is proven, and the suspected cause was wrong. The menus are fzf (`ui.Picker.menu` runs fzf), not Bubble Tea. fzf's light renderer waits up to 100 ms (ESCDELAY) after an Esc and reads the Esc and the key after it as that key with Alt: Esc then Down is `alt-down`, Esc then Enter `ctrl-alt-m` (0.38) or `alt-enter`, Esc then Ctrl-R `ctrl-alt-r`. A screen with nothing bound to them drops the Esc. Real fzf 0.38 in tmux (scratch probe): Esc then Down 20, 50 and 80 ms apart, and `\e\e[B` in one write, left fzf open; 100 and 130 ms apart, Esc was honoured (exit 130). The same for Esc then Enter, q, Ctrl-R, Up, Tab and j. Binding the joined keys to abort made every case leave. tmux 3.3a with its default escape-time (500 ms) joins Esc and Down typed 100 ms apart into one write `\e\e[B` for the program in the pane; at escape-time 10 they stay apart. Bubble Tea v2 (ultraviolet) decodes `\e\e[B` as alt+down too (its escape wait is 50 ms), and gum 0.17 dropped the Esc of a one-write `\e\e[B` (it honoured Esc then Down 20 ms apart).
Class: an Esc that a key reader joins to the next key, which a screen that leaves on Esc then ignores, so the person's next keys act on a screen they think they left.

## 14:17 · decision · T10.4   — gum stays: a lost Esc reaches nothing destructive once escape-time is 10

gum stays (option c), decided by delegated review on the owner's instruction. The question: fzf, Bubble Tea v2 and gum read a one-write `\e\e[B` as alt-down and drop the Esc; fzf and the Go screens are bound (Task 10.4), gum takes no bindings. Should gum be replaced everywhere (a), only where a lost Esc harms (b), or kept behind tmux `escape-time` 10 (c)?

Facts found:

- Every gum call is Go, through `internal/ui` (`Gum.Confirm`, `Input`, `Choose`; `Style`, `Box`, `Explain` read no key). The `table.go` rule makes `internal/ui` gum's only owner. A word-boundary grep over `files/`, `scripts/`, `playbooks/`, `lib/` finds no bash caller, so the bash-limits rule ("no interactive UI in bash") is already met.
- The 10-26 finding entry: gum 0.17 lost the Esc only of a one-write `\e\e[B`; it honoured Esc then Down 20 ms apart. tmux at its default `escape-time` joined keys typed apart into one write; at `escape-time` 10 keys more than 10 ms apart stay apart. A person cannot type two keys inside 10 ms, and a terminal without tmux never joins them, so after the deploy only `tmux send-keys` with both keys in one call, or a paste, reaches the defect.
- Site by site: a lost Esc on a gum confirm leaves it open, and the next Enter takes the default. The default-yes confirms are the backup offer, taking sessions off a team (they keep running), the LXC member write and apply (sudo asks a password next), the wizard's final yes (sudo), sign-in, the bus-plays update (sudo) and starting a session. Delete-for-good makes the person type the team's name (`takedown.chooseFate`); coordinator takeover and Element consent default to No; the fate menu starts on "stay running". A lost Esc in an input types garbage that a typed-name gate rejects. Nothing destructive is reachable.
- (b) therefore has no members; (a) would rebuild the questions in Bubble Tea v2, which joins Esc the same way (its wait is 50 ms), repeating the fzf binding work for no reachable gain.

Decision (c): keep gum, rely on `escape-time` 10 and the gates above. Standing condition written into PLAN.md: a destructive gum confirm defaults to No or sits behind a typed name. Task 10.5 added: the host agent checks one gum confirm, choose and input in tmux after the deploy, Esc then Down sent 20 ms apart. Revisit if that check shows gum losing an Esc from keys typed apart. Full analysis kept in the worktree's `untracked/scratch/gum-decision.md` (not tracked).
