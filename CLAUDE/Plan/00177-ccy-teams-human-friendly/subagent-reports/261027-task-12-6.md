# Task 12.6: the flaky real-gum drain test (sub-agent report)

Branch `fix/00177-flaky-drain-test`, from F44 at 0cb8e7532. Only
`src-go/internal/ui/drain_pty_test.go` changed, so no host run is needed.

## Measured before

The test binary was built from F44 and run under CPU load: 44 busy loops on 22 cores.

- `/1ms` alone, `-count=50`: 7 of 50 failed.
- All of `TestOnTheRealGum`, `-count=30`: 2 of 30 `/1ms` runs failed. The other gaps and the
  type-ahead test did not fail.
- Every failure was the same: `the screen never showed "What happens to it?"`, after the
  10 s wait. Step 2 never drew. No failure was a wrong choice.

That makes 9 of 80 runs of the 1 ms case red under load (about 11%).

## The cause: the test's own timing, not the settle

The test wrote Esc, slept 1 ms, then wrote Down. Under load gum confirm had not yet read the Esc
when the Down arrived, so it read `ESC ESC [ B` in one read. Bubble Tea parses that as Alt+Down,
which the confirm ignores, so the confirm never ended.

A probe proved this: Esc and Down written in one write. The confirm did not end in 3 s, 3 of 3
runs. The probe was a scratch test and was deleted.

So it was neither of the races the task suggested. The Down did not reach step 2 instead of
gum 1, and the screen was not read before the draw. The 10 s draw timeout was not too tight
either: the draw never came. The settle and the production code are not at fault.

## The fix (test only)

A small runner, `confirmEnds`, wraps `execx.OS` under the hold. It closes a channel when the
gum confirm's `Run` returns. `takeDownSteps` waits for that channel (10 s bound), then sleeps
the gap, then sends the Down.

The gap now counts from the confirm's end, not from the Esc's write. A Down can no longer join
the Esc. It always lands where the host's did: after the confirm ended and before step 2 drew.
On an idle machine the confirm ends about 3 ms after the Esc (Task 12.5's timing), so the gaps
still cover the host's 1 to 40 ms after the Esc. The type-ahead test uses the same helper and
still proves that a key typed after a plain answer reaches step 2.

## The data race (found by the coordinator's qa-all, which runs `-race`)

On F44, qa-all's go-qa failed `/1ms` with "never showed" and also `race detected`. A failing
`waitFor` calls `Fatalf`, and gumPty's cleanup then closes the master and the terminal. The gum
goroutine started under `Held` had read the terminal's fd (`os.StartProcess`), but it was still
running, so nothing ordered its use of the fd before the close. The race is only on the failure
path, but it turns one failure into two, and it would hide a real one.

- **Red:** a temporary probe drove `takeDownSteps` down its failure path. It answered with Esc
  and Down in one write, so the confirm never ends. Under `-race` it reported `WARNING: DATA RACE`.
- **Fix:** `takeDownSteps` registers a cleanup that waits for the gum goroutine to finish.
  Cleanups run last in, first out, so it runs before gumPty's close. `t.Context()` is cancelled
  before the cleanups run, which sends gum its SIGTERM, so the wait ends.
- **Green:** the same probe, 3 of 3, fails with its own message only and reports no race. The
  probe was deleted.

## Measured after (with `-race` and without)

- `/1ms` alone, `-count=50`, under the same CPU load: 0 of 50 failed, both without `-race` and
  with it.
- All of `TestOnTheRealGum`, `-count=30`, under CPU load: 150 of 150 gap runs passed, and

- `-count=20` beside a parallel `go test -count=1 ./...` of the whole module: 100 of 100 passed.

- For comparison, F44's test with `-race` under the same load: 2 of 50 `/1ms` runs failed.
  Both were "never showed", and the race was reported.

## Still red without the settle

For the check, `ttyecho.New` was given `Settle: nil`, then reverted. The test binary was built
from the new test. First without `-race`:

- Idle, `-count=10`: every run red. The 5, 10, 20 and 40 ms gaps failed 10 of 10 each, and
  1 ms failed 6 of 10. Each failure was `step 2 took "Take it down, keep its data"`.
- Under load, `-count=10`: every run red. 20 and 40 ms failed 10 of 10 and 10 ms failed 9 of 10.
  The 1 and 5 ms gaps passed under load. This is the same window 12.5 found: a Down sent very
  early is caught even by an immediate flush.
- With `-race`, after the race fix: 10 of 10 runs red idle, and 10 of 10 red under load. No race
  was reported.

The verdict is deterministic in both directions: green with the settle, and red on every run
without it.
