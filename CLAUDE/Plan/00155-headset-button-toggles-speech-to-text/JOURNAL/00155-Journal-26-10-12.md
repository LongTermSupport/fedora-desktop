## 09:04 · finding · —   — Button: KEY_PLAYCD in A2DP, AT+CHUP in HFP (consumed by PipeWire)

Live capture on the host with the owner pressing the button (OpenRun Pro). Tools:
a read-only evdev reader on the headset's `(AVRCP)` node, and `btmon` (bluez,
already installed) for the raw ACL traffic. Raw captures stay under `untracked/`.

**A2DP (idle, music mode): detectable as a key.** Each single press arrived as an
AVCTP passthrough `PLAY` (op 0x44 pressed, 0xC4 released) and as `KEY_PLAYCD`
down/up on the evdev node. The headset sends `PLAY` both to start and to pause
music, so H1 is refuted in its alternating form: one code, `KEY_PLAYCD`, for every
single press. GNOME's media handling toggled the player as well.

**HFP (headset mic recording, i.e. during a dictation): not a key.** A press during a
dictation arrived only on the HFP RFCOMM link as `AT+CHUP` (hang up). PipeWire's
native HFP backend (inside WirePlumber) answered `ERROR`, because there is no call, and
nothing else happened. The evdev node received nothing. So F5 is confirmed, and the
stop press is invisible to any evdev listener.

Not yet captured: a double press and a long press. The owner's run covered single
presses in A2DP and one press in HFP.

Consequence for the design: a listener can start a dictation from evdev
(`KEY_PLAYCD`), but stopping one needs the `AT+CHUP` that WirePlumber consumes. Open
research: whether PipeWire 1.6's bluez5 backend exposes call-control commands
(its telephony D-Bus interface, or a WirePlumber hook), or whether `AT+CHUP` can be
observed by another supported route. Raw HCI monitoring needs root and is a last
resort.

## 09:21 · finding · —   — disable-dummy-call: button OK in HFP but the OpenRun mic dies -- rejected

Tested RESEARCH-hfp-button.md's recommendation on the host: `bluez5.disable-dummy-call = true` set for the OpenRun Pro only, through a `monitor.bluez.rules` match on
`device.alias` (the play run with the device list as an extra var; never committed).

- **The button part worked.** During a dictation (headset in `headset-head-unit`), each
  press arrived as AVRCP `KEY_PLAYCD` on the evdev node, and no `AT+CHUP` was sent. So
  with the dummy call off, this headset keeps its AVRCP button in HFP.
- **The microphone part failed: rejected.** With the dummy call off, the headset's SCO
  audio stopped about 0.5 s after each stream started. `spa.bluez5: Failure in Bluetooth audio transport` was logged every time, and a 24 s dictation captured
  17,606 bytes (expected about 770 KB). The owner's dictation was lost ("No speech
  detected"). This is the timeout the dummy call exists to prevent (upstream #1744).
- **Reverting the rule and restarting WirePlumber did not recover it.** The bluez
  transport stayed broken until the headset was disconnected and reconnected. After
  that, a 6 s capture gave 191 KB and logged no errors. The research's warning was
  right: changing this while the headset is in HFP can leave it stuck.

So `disable-dummy-call` is not usable for the OpenRun Pro. A device in the
dictation profile keeps the dummy call, and the stop press in HFP stays an
`AT+CHUP` that PipeWire answers with `ERROR`. Remaining routes, from the research's
fallbacks: (a) follow the `spa.bluez5.native` info-level journal line `RFCOMM receive command but modem not available: AT+CHUP` (not an API; it does not name the device;
it needs a scoped log level); (b) a root HCI monitor service. The owner decides
between them; neither is built.

## 09:45 · finding · —   — Only single presses leave the OpenRun; dummy AVRCP player changes nothing

**Only a single press leaves the OpenRun. Double, triple and long presses send nothing, in
both modes, with or without a dummy media player.** Captured on the host with `btmon` (raw
HCI) and the read-only evdev reader on the headset's `(AVRCP)` node, while the owner pressed:
single, double and long, with music off and with music playing; then again during a
dictation (HFP).

- **A2DP, stock config.** Each single press: AVCTP passthrough `PLAY` and `KEY_PLAYCD`
  down/up. Double and long presses: no AVCTP frame and no key event at all. The headset
  handles those gestures itself (or ignores them) and tells the computer nothing.
- **HFP.** Single press: `AT+CHUP` (see 09:04). Double and long: nothing on RFCOMM.
- **A2DP with `bluez5.dummy-avrcp-player = true`** (a WirePlumber monitor property that
  registers a media player per adapter, because some headsets only send next/previous track
  when the computer advertises one). Deployed via `play-hd-audio.yml` with an extra var,
  headset reconnected, same presses. Result unchanged: two `KEY_PLAYCD` presses (the two
  singles); the owner confirms the double press skipped nothing. The property was removed
  and the play re-run.

Consequence for the design: the gesture-to-key mapper can only ever see **one** gesture from
this device, the single press. "Double press → Insert, single stays play/pause" is not
possible with the OpenRun. What remains is single press → Insert on devices in the
dictation-device profile (losing play/pause on those devices only), which is the owner's
call.

Side effect seen: restarting WirePlumber while the headset was connected left the mic
delivering about 15% of the expected bytes (19 KB in 4 s against 127 KB) until the headset
was disconnected and reconnected. Recorded under Plan 00172.
