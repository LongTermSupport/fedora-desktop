## 11:49 · finding · —   — Slow UniFi Wi-Fi was a bad association; reconnect 39 -> 400+ Mbit/s; iwlwifi firmware crashes suspected

**The UniFi network's "single-digit Mbps" was one bad association, not the radios or the
wiring; a fresh connection to the same access point ran ten times faster.** Owner report:
the UniFi network slow again, the laptop moved to the ISP router's Wi-Fi.

Read-only triage (controller started on demand, `unifi-api.bash stat/device`):

- Radios: three U7 Pro access points, every channel fixed and running as set (2.4 GHz
  1/6/11 at 20 MHz; 5 GHz 36/52/100 at 80 MHz; 6 GHz 21/37/85 at 160 MHz), channel
  utilisation 1-3% on 5 and 6 GHz. Uplinks: all three wired at 2.5 Gbps full duplex,
  1 rx error in total, no mesh.
- DSCP probe (`wifi-dscp-probe.bash`) to an access point, both switches and the gateway:
  0% loss in every queue, so not the 2026-10-10 fault.
- Laptop on the office access point's 6 GHz (160 MHz, -54 dBm, link 1.7 Gbps): 4-stream
  download from a London server **39 Mbit/s**, the ISP router's own Wi-Fi 613 on the same
  line. Pings to the access point itself, idle, spiked to about 105 ms (one beacon
  interval) every few seconds. Wi-Fi power save off: unchanged (46 Mbit/s, same spikes),
  so not power save; restored on.
- A reconnect (NetworkManager re-activation; it chose the same 6 GHz access point): the
  spikes gone (1.6 ms average to the access point) and **390, 461, 415 Mbit/s**.

So the radio, channel and wiring are fine and the fault lives in the association state.
Suspect: the laptop side. The Intel AX211 (Meteor Lake CNVi, iwlwifi firmware 89) logged
"Microcode SW error" five times in seven days (Oct 6, 9 twice, 10, 12), each a firmware
restart; a degraded state after one is a known iwlwifi failure mode. Not yet proven: the
next slow episode should be caught before reconnecting (link stats, firmware log, ping to
the access point) to tie it to a firmware event.

Side findings, the ISP router's network: its 5 GHz shares channel 100 with one UniFi 5 GHz
radio; it runs 80 MHz, which caps the laptop at a 1.2 Gbps link (about 600 Mbit/s seen,
the internet line's level). Set to 160 MHz it still runs 80 (it offers 160 but backs off),
and it would not leave channel 100 (likely held by its own mesh backhaul). Owner: happy to
stay on the UniFi network, so the ISP router is left alone.
