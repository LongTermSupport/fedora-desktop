## 16:59 · finding · —   — qa-changed: a member/engine test binary spun for 24 minutes under the failpoint tag, not reproduced

A finding from `qa-changed` on the cross-network branch: the Go gate ended at its 30 minute limit with every detector green and no test failure printed, because one test binary never ended. It was `member/engine`'s, built with the `busfailpoints` tag and run by the `teamchat-failpoints-test` stage (`go test -race -shuffle=on -count=2`): 24 minutes at 100% of one core when I found it, which is what a test spinning in a polling loop looks like. The qa-changed process had already reported the timeout and exited, but the `go tool go-qa` command and the test run under it were still running (the `go` command was its own process group leader and its parent was init) and held a core until I ended that group. `qa/internal/proc` kills the group of the process it started (`Setpgid` and a `-pid` SIGKILL), so the likely cause is that a layer below it starts go-qa in a group of its own, which the kill does not reach; I have not traced it. It is a second defect of its own: a stage that timed out is reported but not ended.

What I established. The same package, tag and flags pass alone in 24 s: 18 runs under random shuffle seeds, 800 runs of the two tests that later dumps showed, and 60 runs of one of them alone. Under heavy parallel load (ten or twelve copies at once on eight cores) a run takes far longer than 100 s, and a goroutine dump taken then shows a test inside `world.until`: that is the usual state of a slow run, not evidence of a hang. My first reading of those dumps (two different tests "stuck", seeds that "reproduce") was wrong: a diagnostic build that fails an `until` after 20 s did not fire once, so none of those runs was stuck. I have not reproduced the 24 minute spin and I do not know its cause.

What is known about the shape. `world.until` (`engine_test.go`), `waitFor` (`send_test.go`) and the loop in `recv_test.go` that advances the fake clock until a wait answers poll with `runtime.Gosched()` and no bound of their own. The proof, send and delivery loops each sleep in a `select` on a poke channel, a timer and the context, so a wakeup that is lost turns a failing test into a spin that lasts until `go test`'s own 30 minute timeout, burning a core that slows every test beside it. `dworld.settle` and `TestWaitingWhileAWaitRuns` test `t.Context().Err()`, which is not cancelled while the test body runs, so neither is a bound. Nothing in this branch's diff touches the package and the stage passed in the two earlier runs of this work, so the cause is in code that came in with the bus v2 merge, or is a rare ordering.

What I did not do. I added no bound to those loops and narrowed or skipped nothing. `CLAUDE/GoProjectRules.md` (`project_realtime_bound`) counts a poll budget that ends in `t.Fatal` as correct, so a budget is allowed; I held back because I cannot yet say how long a wait may take under load without making a slow run a false failure, and because a hang in CI is itself the diagnostic (`go test` prints every goroutine when its timeout fires, and CI keeps that log). If it recurs there, the dump names the test and the loop. Whether to add a budget now, and how to hold the class (an unbounded polling loop in a test), is the owner's call.
