## Design as implemented

- **The record is per seat** (owner ruling on #417):
  `<checkout>/.claude/ccy/seat-conversations/<team>/<seat>.json`, one file per seat,
  `{v, seat, team, session_id, source, written_ms}`, strict decode, atomic write. It lives in
  the checkout (ccy's ignored directory), not in any session's state, so the end of a session
  cannot remove it. Writers, all on the host: the launch (`seat-resume`), the session's end
  (`bus-disconnected`), the repair (`--set`). Sources: launch, fresh, rebind, relaunch, set, exit.
- **`conversation.ForSeats`** reads the seat records first. Every named seat that has a record
  must name the same id; a named seat with none is allowed when another has one and is
  returned in `Unrecorded` (the launch then records it). So a subset or superset of a
  session's seats resolves. With no seat record at all, the per-session lookup of #409 answers
  as before (migration of existing installs). Every refusal is a `*SeatError` with one of the
  four cases: `no-session`, `no-conversation`, `ambiguous` (seats disagree, two sessions, or one
  id recorded twice), `no-transcript` (names `<checkout>/.claude/ccy/projects/-workspace/<id>.jsonl`).
- **`ccy-teams internal seat-resume`** (new, `src-go/internal/teams/seatresume.go`) decides a
  seated launch's conversation. The launcher's `ccy_seats_resume` (`lib/agent-bus-seats.bash`)
  calls it right after the seat check, with CLAUDE_ARGS. ccy's `--fresh` and `--rebind` reach
  CLAUDE_ARGS through the parse's last branch. The verb takes them out of the words before the
  launcher's `--` (`--before=<n>`) and prints the new count, then the new CLAUDE_ARGS (NUL
  separated). This keeps both bash files within their size records.
  - Plain launch: `--resume <recorded id>` added, unrecorded seats recorded. If no id can be
    named: exit 64 with the case, then `Repair: ccy-teams seats conversation <seats> --set <id>`
    and `Fresh: ccy --teams <seats> --fresh`.
  - `--fresh`: a new v4 UUID, `--session-id <id>` added, recorded for every seat at once, so
    the next plain launch resumes it.
  - `--resume <id>` (or `--session-id <id>`): accepted and recorded when it equals the
    record, when there is no resumable record, with `--rebind`, or on a relaunch
    (`CCY_SESSION_RESTORE` or `CCY_RESTART_RELAUNCH_SESSION` set). Any other id: exit 64,
    naming both ids, the plain form and `--rebind`.
  - `--continue`/`-c`: exit 64, naming `ccy --teams <seats>` and `--fresh`, with no id form.
  - Bare `--resume` (the picker): nothing added, nothing recorded.
  - Contradictions are refused with 64: `--fresh` with any selector, `--rebind` with no id,
    two selectors, a malformed list.
- **Session end**: `bus-disconnected` first calls `conversation.SyncSession`, on both systems
  and whatever the exit status. The session's seats come from its own state (teamchat
  `desired.json`, else the v1 keeper's report) while that still exists. Its own conversation
  record is read as a restore reads it, transcript included, and written into those seats.
  If there is nothing to carry, that is said and the records are left alone. A failed write is
  an error, and the session is still ended.
- **Restore** (`conversation.StartPlan`, used by `ccy-sessions restore`, its `--check`,
  `declare` and `ccy-teams restart`): a seated ccy record whose own conversation record is
  missing, or has no transcript, is resumed by its seats' record. It is never started fresh
  while the seats name a conversation. Otherwise the seat lookup's reason is added to the error.
  `FreshArgs` of a seated ccy record adds `--fresh`.
- `--fresh` and `--rebind` are drop flags: a restore never replays them. They are listed in
  `registry.dropFlags` and in its bash twin `CCY_REGISTRY_DROP_FLAGS`.
- `ccy-teams seats conversation <seats> --set <id>` checks that the transcript is in this
  checkout, then records the id. The query's refusal also names both ways on.

### The incident (R4)

A session's first join reaches the launcher as a relaunch carrying `--resume <its id>` (the
supervisor's relaunch or a restore). `seat-resume` records that id for the seats. That is the
"carry the existing id into the seat record" step. It does not read the v1 path under the
session's name: rule `project_conversation_record` forbids falling back from one system's
record to the other, because a reused name is an earlier session's. The clean `/exit` then
syncs the seat record before the teamchat state is removed. After that, both
`ccy-teams seats conversation 4@<team>` and a restore of the record answer from the seat record.

### Seat 4 against seat 5 (the coordinator's question)

The coordinator's report from the team (A6): seat 5 survived a clean `/exit` and restore;
seat 4 got a fresh-start proposal. Seat 4's session existed before it joined a team, and its
teamchat record says source `resume`; seat 5's says `startup`.

**The source is not the cause in the code.** No reader looks at the source field:
`state.DecodeConversation` accepts startup, resume, clear and compact alike, and `readRecorded`,
`StartPlan` and `ForSeats` read only `session_id`, `written_ms` and the transcript.

**The guess about how a seat got its record matches the old code's shape.** Before this change
there was no seat record at all. A seat's conversation could be named only through a session
whose current membership (`desired.json` / `applied.json`) held the seat, plus that session's
own record. A restore read only the session's own record. Seat 5 survived because its own
teamchat record and its transcript were both still there at restore. Seat 4's dry run was
"fresh", and `StartPlan` gives that only for a record marked fresh-if-no-conversation whose
own record is `errNotRecorded` (no `conversation.json` at the seat store's
`sessions/<name>/`) or `errNoTranscript` (the recorded id has no `<id>.jsonl` in the checkout).

**Which of the two it was cannot be told from the code.** It needs the host's files:
`ls <state>/…/sessions/<name>/conversation.json` and `<checkout>/.claude/ccy/projects/-workspace/<id>.jsonl`.
Plausible routes, all unverified:

- The record was written under a name other than the registry record's. A session that existed
  before joining is relaunched by the supervisor, so its link's session name may not be the
  one the play restored.
- A `--resume` start was recorded with an id whose transcript is elsewhere.

**Both routes are covered now, whichever it was.**

- A relaunch into a seat with no record records the id it resumes:
  `TestSeatResumeARelaunchIntoAnUnrecordedSeatRecordsIt`.
- The exit carries a `resume`-sourced record into the seat, and the seat record outlives the
  session's state: `TestSyncSessionCarriesASessionThatResumedIntoItsSeat`.
- A restore whose own record is missing, or has no transcript, resumes by the seat record
  instead of starting fresh: `TestStartPlanResumesASeatedRecordByItsSeatRecord`.

### Defence Before Fix

The class is "a record a later resume needs is held only in per-session state that the
session's end removes". It is a lifecycle property across processes, and no static detector
can express it. So it was fixed conventionally: the record moved to a store that no end path
deletes, and tests pin that the exit carries the conversation over before forgetting the session.

## Callers checked

- `ccy-sessions restore` / `restore --check` / `declare` (`internal/sessions/restore.go` via
  `StartPlan`): resume by the session's own id, falling back to the seat record. They run
  with `CCY_SESSION_RESTORE=1`, so a different id moves the record and needs no `--rebind`.
  A fresh start adds `--fresh`.
- `ccy-teams restart` (`internal/teams/restart/jobs.go`): the same `StartPlan`, run with
  `CCY_SESSION_RESTORE=1`. The test for the fresh form was updated to expect `--fresh`.
- Supervisor relaunch (`lib/restart-request.bash`, `ccy-teams internal restart-args`): it
  passes `--resume <CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID>`, and `CCY_RESTART_RELAUNCH_SESSION` is set, so the
  relaunch is accepted and the record moves. It replays registry args, which never carry
  `--fresh` or `--rebind`.
- `ccy-teams create` (`create/wizard.go`): the first session of a new team now execs
  `ccy --teams <team> --fresh`.
- `seats.StartCcy` (ccy-teams "Start a ccy session" on a seat row): no change. It resumes the
  seat, or refuses with the repair and `--fresh`.
- Scripts that relied on a fresh start, now given `--fresh`:
  - Plan 00161 `_acceptance-u20.inc.bash` (its seated headless launch)
  - Plan 00201 `_acceptance-m7.inc.bash`
  - Plan 00194 `_acceptance-goodbye.inc.bash`
- Left alone: the 00161 refused-value case, which is refused at the seat check, and the 00201
  `chooser-teams-up.bash` hand chooser.

## Live proof needed (host; not attempted here)

Deploy: `./CLAUDE/Plan/meta-deploy.bash`. Its queued `play-claude-yolo.yml` installs ccy
3.124.44 and `ccy-teams`.

1. **Default resume.** In a team checkout with a seat that has a conversation, run
   `ccy --teams <n>@<team>`, take one turn, `/exit`, then run `ccy --teams <n>@<team>` again.
   Accept: the launch says "resuming the conversation <id> that the seats ... recorded", and
   Claude shows the earlier turns; `.claude/ccy/seat-conversations/<team>/<n>.json` holds that
   id with source `exit` or `launch`.
2. **R4 incident.** Hot-add a running no-team session to a team (`ccy-teams session add`), let
   it relaunch, take a turn, then `/exit` cleanly.
   - Accept: `ccy-teams seats conversation <n>@<team>` prints the session's id;
     `ccy-sessions restore --dry-run` does not propose a fresh start for it.
   - Accept: `ccy --teams <n>@<team>` resumes it.
3. **Refusal.** Run `ccy --teams <new-seat>@<team>` for a seat that never ran. Accept: exit
   64, the case "no session record ... holds", and both lines `Repair: ...--set <id>` and
   `Fresh: ccy --teams ... --fresh`. Then `ccy --teams <new-seat>@<team> --fresh` starts, and a
   plain relaunch after one turn resumes it.
4. **Rebind.** Run `ccy --teams <n>@<team> --resume <other-id>`. Accept: exit 64 naming both
   ids and `--rebind`. With `--rebind` it starts, and the seat file holds `<other-id>`.
5. **Reboot restore.** Reboot with a seated session running. Accept: `ccy-sessions verify-restore` shows it resumed on its conversation, not fresh.
6. **Team restart.** Run `ccy-teams restart <team>`. Accept: each seat comes back on its own
   conversation, with no 64 refusal from a mismatched id.

## NOT proven

- No live launch, relaunch, restore or reboot was run. Everything above is unit tests, plus
  the difftest sandbox driving the real bash launcher with a faked `ccy-teams`.
- That the supervisor's first-join relaunch carries `--teams` together with
  `--resume <id>`. It was read from the code, not observed.
- That a teamchat session's `desired.json` still exists when `bus-disconnected` runs. The
  sync reads it before releasing seats, but the order against the link's own teardown inside
  the container was not observed live. If it is already gone, the v1 report is tried, and
  otherwise nothing is carried (this is said on stderr and in the disconnect log).
- Behaviour changes that UX may need to soften:
  - A person who picks a brand-new seat in the bare `ccy --teams` chooser now gets the 64
    refusal and must add `--fresh`. U3 (seat choice) may want the chooser to pass `--fresh`
    for a seat it creates.
  - A seated `ccy --teams` session that `/clear`s and is then killed (no clean exit, no
    restore) leaves the seat on the pre-`/clear` id until its next exit or restore.
  - `--fresh` or `--rebind` without `--teams` reaches claude's flag check and is refused
    there, with claude's message rather than ccy's: no bash room is left for a ccy-worded
    refusal.
  - The `ccy-teams create` summary still suggests a plain `ccy --teams <team>` for later
    sessions.
- Host `cc --teams` (`cc-teams.bash`) is not covered; it has its own launch path.
