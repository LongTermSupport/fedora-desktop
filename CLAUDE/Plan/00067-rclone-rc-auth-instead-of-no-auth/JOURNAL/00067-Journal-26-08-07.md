## 06:25 · finding · — — correction: yesterday's work was filed in the wrong day-file

The two entries timestamped `2026-08-07 07:05` and `2026-08-07 07:12` were
appended to `00067-Journal-26-07-30.md`. That is wrong twice over:

1. **Wrong file.** Work done on 26-08-07 belongs in a `26-08-07` day-file. The
   convention exists so "the next agent's entry point is the last entry of the
   newest day-file" actually resolves — with the mis-filing, the newest day-file
   was dated eight days before the newest work.
2. **Wrong header grammar.** Those entries used a full `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` stamp
   instead of the bare `HH:MM` the grammar specifies (the date lives in the
   filename, so repeating it in the header is redundant and breaks the parse).

Per the append-only rule the originals are **left untouched** — this entry is the
correction, not an edit. Their content stands; only their location is wrong.
Read `00067-Journal-26-07-30.md` from `## 2026-08-07 07:05` onward for what was
actually done: `triage.bash`, `acceptance.bash` and `deploy.bash` written, QA
green, committed as `4f4aa1e`.

Root cause worth remembering: the day-file was opened by `mkplan.bash` on the day
the plan was created, and a later session resumed by appending to "the plan's
journal" rather than to "today's journal". Resuming a plan after a gap means
opening a new day-file first.
